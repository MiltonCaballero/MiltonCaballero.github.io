Networks that hold up when things go wrong. Advanced optimization techniques for community resilience, fairness and network infrastructure.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Data Science and Analytics at the University of Oklahoma, advised by Dr. Andrés D. González in the Netsys Research Lab.

My work asks how transportation, logistics and interdependent infrastructure systems should be planned when demand is uncertain and disruptions spread — and how to make those decisions both efficient and fair.

Research

Three threads run through my work, all built on large-scale mathematical programs solved in Python and Gurobi and tested on real infrastructure data.

Synchromodal freight under uncertainty

Two-stage stochastic and bi-objective models that allocate containers and power units across road, rail and water modes when demand is only known as a distribution.

Distributionally robust network flow

Data-driven ambiguity sets for minimum-cost flow problems where a single failure cascades through the network, rather than staying local.

Fairness and resilience

Graph-theoretic formulations for targeted arc additions, and the question of when equitable service allocation should take priority under scarcity.
